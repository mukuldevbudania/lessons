Lesson 0007 · Finishing the Pixels

Clean It Up

Take your fast, low-res draft clips to delivery resolution and smooth frame rate — the cheap polish that happens after generation, not during.

In Lesson 0006 you generated at 480p and short durations on purpose — to iterate fast and not pay for quality on takes you might reject. Now that your shots are approved, you lift them to delivery spec. ~8 min. Your win: one approved clip at 1080p, smooth.

1. Why generation and finishing are separate steps

Generating at full 1080p / high-fps on the Spark is slow and wasteful when you're still deciding. So the pipeline splits it: generate cheap, finish once. Upscaling and frame interpolation are comparatively light, deterministic, and run on your final picks only.

2. The two moves

MoveWhat it doesTool (in ComfyUI)Spark fit
Upscale480p/720p → 1080p (or higher), restoring detailA latent/model upscaler node (e.g. an ESRGAN-family or diffusion upscaler)✅ Trivial — light
InterpolateFills in frames: e.g. 12–16fps → smooth 24–30fpsFrame-interpolation node — RIFE or FILM✅ Trivial — light

Both are small, light models — they fit the Spark trivially. The only cost is wall-clock on a full set of clips, so batch them. (Spark-fit reference.)

Order matters. Interpolate then upscale is usually cheaper (fewer large frames to upscale), but either order works — the point is both happen after the shot is locked, never as part of the slow generation loop.

3. Keep it a batch job too

Still the Spark. Upscaling a full set of clips is also minutes-per-clip work. Treat it like the video renders: queue the whole approved set and let it run. You should never be sitting watching an upscale bar — that time is for prompting the next thing or writing in Resolve.

4. Don't over-polish

Target exactly your delivery spec from the brief — if it's 9:16 social, 1080×1920 at 30fps is plenty; 4K is wasted render time nobody will see on a phone. Match the platform, stop there.

5. Your win — one clip at spec ✍️

1
Take one approved 480p clip from Lesson 0006.
2
Run frame interpolation (RIFE/FILM) to your target fps, then upscale to your delivery resolution.
3
Eyeball it: detail restored without a plastic/over-sharpened look? motion smooth without warping artifacts? If yes, batch the rest.
Done = one approved shot at your exact delivery resolution and fps, clean enough to sit on a timeline. The remaining shots are the same batch recipe.

6. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

Why generate at 480p and upscale later instead of generating at 1080p directly?

You iterate on many takes but keep few. Generating cheap and finishing only the approved picks avoids paying full-resolution render cost on takes you reject — crucial on the bandwidth-bound Spark.

What does frame interpolation give you, and which tools?

Smoother motion by synthesizing in-between frames (e.g. 12→24fps). RIFE or FILM.

What resolution should you target?

Exactly your brief's delivery spec and no more — 4K for a 9:16 phone ad is wasted render time.
