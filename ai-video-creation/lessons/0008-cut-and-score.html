Lesson 0008 · The Edit

Cut & Score

Assemble your clips into a timeline that follows the beat sheet, and give it sound — because the edit is half of what "cinematic" means.

You have finished clips (0007). Now they become an ad. This is a DaVinci Resolve lesson — no GPU generation, just craft. ~12 min. Your win: a cut timeline with sound — your shots in order, paced to the beat sheet, with music and at least rough VO/SFX.

1. The edit carries the energy

Two editors can cut the same footage into a flat montage or a gripping spot. Pacing — how long each shot holds, where the cuts land relative to the music — is where "cinematic" is won or lost. Resolve Free is more than enough for a 20s spot. official Resolve training (free)

2. Resolve's pages, in the order you'll touch them

PageYou do
MediaImport your finished clips + any music/SFX. Set project to your delivery resolution/fps.
Cut / EditLay shots in beat-sheet order; trim; set pacing. This lesson lives here.
FairlightAudio: music bed, voiceover, sound effects, levels.

3. Cut to the beats

1
Drop shots onto the timeline in shot-list order. Don't trim yet — just see the shape.
2
Lay your music bed under it. Find the beat/downbeats.
3
Trim each shot so cuts land on the music and each beat gets its allotted seconds from the beat sheet. Hook short and punchy; let the payoff breathe.
4
Watch it end to end. Does it read as hook→problem→product→payoff→CTA? If a shot doesn't earn its screen time, cut it.

4. Sound is not optional

Silent AI video reads as a tech demo; sound makes it an ad. Even a simple music bed + one clean VO line + two SFX transforms perceived quality more than another re-render would. Do a rough pass now; polish levels after the picture is locked.

How the voice fits — the thing beginners worry about

Your clips have no lip-sync (Wan 2.2 animated motion, not mouths). That's fine, because your voice is a voiceover, not dialogue (you decided this back in Lesson 0004). A VO rides over the visuals — exactly like the narrator in most ads — so there's no mouth to match. To place it:

1
Record or generate the VO as one audio clip. Drop it on its own Fairlight track.
2
Slide it so key lines land on the right shots (the product name as the product appears, the CTA on the final beat).
3
Duck the music under the VO so words stay clear. Done — the voice "fits" by timing, not by lip-sync.
If you catch yourself needing a mouth to move, stop — that's a talking-to-camera shot, which this pipeline doesn't do. Either recut so that line is VO over a different shot, or park it for milestone 2 (Lesson 0010 — Wan 2.2 S2V, all Spark-fit). Don't fight it on your first spot.
New to Resolve? For this spot you only need the Cut/Edit page (drag, trim, order) and Fairlight (drop audio on tracks, set levels) — a couple of hours of beginner use, not color-science mastery. If even that's a blocker right now, a lighter editor (CapCut, Shotcut) can do a 20s cut too; Resolve is just the better long-term home, and its free grade (next lesson) genuinely matters for AI footage.
Licensing applies to audio too. Your models are commercial-safe — don't undo that with copyrighted music. Use royalty-free / properly licensed tracks and SFX, or generate them. The whole deliverable has to be shippable, not just the visuals.

5. Your win — a cut with sound ✍️

Done = all your shots on one Resolve timeline, in order, trimmed so the pacing follows the beat sheet and cuts sit on the music, with a music bed and rough VO/SFX. It should already feel like a spot, even before the grade.

6. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

Why is the edit described as "half of cinematic"?

Pacing — shot durations and where cuts land against the music — determines a spot's energy as much as the footage does. The same clips can be flat or gripping depending on the cut.

Which Resolve page is for audio, and what's the minimum sound pass?

Fairlight. Minimum: a music bed + one clean VO line + a couple of SFX — enough to move it from "tech demo" to "ad".

Your models are commercial-safe. What else must be licensed for the ad to ship?

The audio — music and SFX. Copyrighted tracks would make the finished ad unshippable despite clean model licensing.

Your clips don't lip-sync. How does the voice still fit?

It's a voiceover, not dialogue — it rides over the visuals and fits by timing (key lines on the right shots, music ducked under it), so there's no mouth to match. A talking-to-camera line would need a lip-sync model (Wan 2.2 S2V) — milestone 2, not this spot.
