Lesson 0010 · Milestone 2

Make the Hero Speak

A talking-to-camera hero — the one thing the base pipeline can't do. Here's the lip-sync path, and every model on it fits your Spark.

Gate: ship milestone 1 first. This is a milestone-2 lesson. Finish a full narration-style spot (Lessons 0001–0009) before you take on lip-sync — it's a real step up in moving parts, and most ads never need it.

In Lesson 0006 you learned Wan 2.2 I2V animates motion, not mouths — which is why your first spot used voiceover. Now you want a hero who speaks on camera. That's a different model family. ~12 min. Your win: one locked keyframe + an audio clip → a lip-synced talking shot.

1. Two families — pick by what you have

You have…You want…Family
A still/keyframe + an audio clipA NEW clip of them talkingAudio→video (generation) — Wan 2.2 S2V
An existing clip + new audioRe-sync the MOUTH on footage you already madeLip-sync (mouth re-sync) — LatentSync / MuseTalk
Which fits this course? You already generate your hero, so the natural path is Wan 2.2 S2V — feed your locked keyframe + the VO, get a talking clip. Reach for the re-sync family only when you have a finished clip whose mouth you need to fix to new audio.

Nothing here fails the fit gate — your 128 GB covers all of it (the 14B models that choke a 32GB gaming card load fine). The split is speed: generation models batch; re-sync models and OmniAvatar 1.3B are fast. Full detail in the Spark-fit reference. Wan 2.2 S2V in ComfyUI

Licensing — the usual two-gate rule. Wan S2V (Apache), LatentSync (Apache), MuseTalk (MIT) all clear for commercial brand work. Wav2Lip and some avatar variants are research/uncertain — check the weight's card before you ship, exactly like the Qwen-Image-2.1 trap in Lesson 0002. Hardware fit is not permission to ship.

3. The recommended path — Wan 2.2 S2V

1
Draft your VO line first (clean audio — generated TTS or recorded).
2
Take your locked keyframe of the hero (from Lesson 0005). Load the Wan2.2 S2V ComfyUI workflow; set keyframe as the reference image and the audio as the driver.
3
Prototype on OmniAvatar 1.3B (fast) to check framing/expression, then run the final on Wan 2.2 S2V 14B as an overnight batch — same rhythm as Lesson 0006.
4
Bring the talking clip back into Resolve (Lesson 0008). It cuts and grades like any other shot.
Done = one shot where your hero speaks a line and the mouth matches, generated on a Spark-fit, commercially-licensed model, dropped onto your timeline.

4. When to NOT use it

Lip-sync adds cost, render time, and a new failure mode (uncanny mouths). If a line works as voiceover over a reactive shot — and most do — keep it VO. Use a talking hero only when the hero speaking to the viewer is the point (a founder, a spokesperson, a testimonial).

5. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

You have a locked keyframe + a VO clip and want a new talking shot. Which family?

Audio→video generation — Wan 2.2 S2V. Mouth re-sync (LatentSync/MuseTalk) is for fixing an existing clip's mouth, not generating a new one.

Do any of these lip-sync models fail to fit the Spark?

No — all fit in 128 GB, including the 14B models that gaming cards can't load. The real difference is speed (batch vs fast), not fit.

Which lip-sync models are commercial-safe, and which needs a license check?

Wan S2V (Apache), LatentSync (Apache), MuseTalk (MIT) are safe. Wav2Lip (and some avatar variants) need verifying before shipping.
