Lesson 0009 · Ship It

Grade & Ship

A unifying color grade makes mismatched AI clips feel like one film — then you export and complete your first brand spot.

This is the finish line. You have a cut, scored timeline (0008). Two things left: make the shots look like they belong to one film, and export. ~10 min. Your win — the whole point of the course: a finished 15–30s brand spot that meets the Definition of Done.

1. Why the grade is non-negotiable for AI footage

Clips from different generations won't match — subtle shifts in color temperature, contrast, and palette shot to shot. A deliberate, consistent color grade is the single move that hides this and binds everything into one coherent look. It's also where "brand mood" literally becomes pixels.

2. The grade, in Resolve's Color page

1
Balance each shot — neutralize wild color casts so every clip starts from a sane baseline (primaries: lift/gamma/gain, white balance).
2
Match shot to shot — use Resolve's shot-match + the scopes (waveform/vectorscope) so skin tones and the key light read consistently across cuts.
3
Apply one look — a single grade/LUT across the whole timeline that expresses your brief's tone (the three adjectives from Lesson 0001). Warm and optimistic? Cool and premium? One look, applied to all.
Balance first, look second. Matching the shots to a neutral baseline before you stylize is what makes the final look land evenly. Stylize mismatched clips and the inconsistency shows straight through the grade.

4. The Definition of Done — check all four ✅

1. The hero character OR product is visibly the same across ≥4 shots.
2. It follows your written brief + shot list — not improvised.
3. It's cut, graded, and sound-designed in Resolve.
4. Every asset is from a commercial-licensed open model (and your audio is licensed too).
Final licensing gate. Before you call it shipped, re-check every weight's license card — licenses change. No FLUX.1 [dev], no Qwen-Image-2.1 (Research), no copyrighted music. A brand ad is commercial; the whole chain has to clear. commercial-safe list

5. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

Why does AI footage specifically need a unifying grade?

Clips from separate generations don't match in color/contrast. One consistent grade hides the mismatch and makes them feel like a single film — and encodes the brand's mood.

Balance or stylize first — and why?

Balance to a neutral baseline first, then apply the look. Stylizing mismatched clips lets the inconsistency show through the grade.

Name the four Definition-of-Done checks.

(1) hero same across ≥4 shots, (2) follows brief + shot list, (3) cut/graded/sound-designed in Resolve, (4) every asset commercial-licensed.
