Lesson 0003 · Consistency

Cast Your Hero

Turn one good still into a repeatable "cast" — the single biggest lever on whether your ad looks like one film or a pile of clips.

You have ComfyUI running and one commercial-safe still (Lesson 0002). Now the real work: making that hero — a character or the product itself — come back identical in shot after shot. This is the stage that decides everything. ~12 min. Your win: a character/product sheet — a multi-angle reference grid you'll match every later frame against.

1. Why the sheet is the whole game

"Cinematic" and "on-brand" both reduce to one word: consistency. A viewer forgives rough motion; they do not forgive a hero whose face, logo, or color changes between cuts. The character sheet / turnaround is your source of truth — your "casting". Every keyframe in Lesson 0005 gets judged against it.

2. Three mechanisms — pick the right one for your hero

There is no single consistency button. In 2026 the reliable approach is a stack, and which layers you need depends on how much your hero recurs. source

All three fit the Spark comfortably — IPAdapter and ControlNet are small adapters, and even training a LoRA fits in 128 GB (just run it overnight). See the Spark-fit reference.

The decision rule. Hero appears in one or two shots, or is a static product? → IPAdapter + a good sheet is enough; don't train. Hero is a recurring character across the whole spot? → it's worth training a LoRA (~15–30 clean reference images, a unique trigger word). ControlNet is orthogonal — add it in Lesson 0005 when you need a specific pose. consistency tiers
Stacking tripwire. LoRA + IPAdapter both pulling hard late in sampling makes the face drift toward the reference. Keep IPAdapter weight moderate and let the LoRA own identity. You'll tune this by eye — fix the seed so you're comparing like with like. why it drifts

4. Your win — build the sheet ✍️

From your Lesson-0002 baseline still, produce a reference grid. Minimum viable sheet:

1
Pick your hero from the Lesson-0001 brief — character or product. One hero.
2
Generate a strong "hero shot" on your commercial-safe model (Qwen-Image 20B / FLUX schnell / SDXL). Fix the seed once you like it.
3
Produce ≥4 angles under the same lighting + palette. Reuse the seed and vary only the angle/expression prompt. Keep the background neutral.
4
Lay them in one grid image. That grid is your sheet — the thing Lesson 0005 matches against.
Done = a single reference grid where a stranger would say "that's the same character/product" across every tile. Not perfect — recognizably one hero.

5. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

Your hero appears in only one shot and is a static product. Train a LoRA?

No. IPAdapter + a clean sheet is enough. Reserve a LoRA for a character recurring across the whole spot, where the ~90% lock pays for the training run.

Why fix the seed while building the sheet?

So you're varying only the thing you mean to (angle, expression) and comparing like with like. A changing seed changes everything, hiding whether your consistency controls are working.

Why prefer Qwen-Image when the hero is a product with a logo?

Qwen-Image is the strongest commercial-safe model at rendering legible text/typography — logos and labels mangle less.
