Lesson 0005 · The Lock

Lock Your Keyframes

Generate one locked still per shot — matched to your sheet — so the slow video step has nothing left to get wrong.

This is the hinge of the whole course. You have a cast (0003) and a shot list (0004). Now you turn each shot-list row into one locked still — the exact frame you'll animate next lesson. Everything about identity, composition, and palette gets resolved here, cheaply, where iteration costs seconds. ~12 min. Your win: a keyframe folder — one locked still per shot.

1. The rule, now operational

Resolve consistency on cheap stills first. Only animate frames you've already locked.

Lesson 0001 stated it; this is where you execute it. On the Spark a video clip costs minutes; a still costs seconds. So you do all your deciding on stills: is it the right hero, the right pose, the right light? Lock yes/no here — never discover a problem in the video step.

2. The control stack for a keyframe

Per shot, you're combining three things from earlier lessons into one generation:

LayerComes fromJob in this frame
IdentityYour LoRA or IPAdapter (0003)Make the hero the same hero
CompositionControlNet (pose/depth/edge)Force the shot's framing & camera angle
ContentThe shot-list prompt (0004)Say what's happening in this beat
Where ControlNet earns its place. IPAdapter/LoRA hold who; ControlNet holds how it's framed. Feed a pose skeleton or depth map so shot 2's wide and shot 3's medium are compositions you chose — not the model's random guess. the LoRA + ControlNet + IPAdapter stack

3. Workflow, per shot

1
Start from your Lesson-0002 image graph. Add the identity layer (LoRA loader or IPAdapter + your sheet) and a ControlNet node for composition.
2
Paste the shot's still-image prompt from the shot list. Set the aspect ratio you locked in 0004.
3
Generate a few seeds. Compare against your sheet: same face/logo? right framing? on-palette?
4
Lock it: save the winner and record its seed + settings. That reproducibility is what lets you regenerate or tweak one frame later without redoing all of them.
5
Repeat for every row. One locked still per shot, named by shot number.
Match the sheet ruthlessly. This is the last cheap place to catch drift. If shot 4's hero looks 90% right, fix it now — it will only get more expensive, and less fixable, once it's a moving clip.

4. Your win — the keyframe folder ✍️

Done = one locked still per shot-list row, each matching your sheet's hero and the shot's framing, each with its seed/settings recorded. Lay them in shot order — that's your animatic in stills. If it already reads as a coherent little story, the video step is now low-risk.

5. Retrieval check (eyes closed first)

Why resolve framing and identity on a still rather than in the video clip?

Stills cost seconds and are fully controllable; a Spark video clip costs minutes and is the least controllable step. Catching a problem on the still is cheap; catching it in video is enormously expensive.

Which layer holds the hero's identity, and which holds the shot's framing?

LoRA/IPAdapter hold identity; ControlNet holds composition/framing. Content comes from the shot-list prompt.

Why record each keyframe's seed and settings?

Reproducibility — you can regenerate or tweak one frame later without redoing the whole set.
