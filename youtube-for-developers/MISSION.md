# Mission

**Learner:** A software developer, comfortable with code and tooling, but **new to YouTube** — has never planned, recorded, edited, or published a video.

**Why this course exists:** To go from "I've never made a video" to confidently running a YouTube channel end to end — planning a video, recording it, editing it, publishing it well, and understanding the levers that actually grow a channel (watch time, click-through, consistency), without drowning in creator-marketing hype.

**Starting point (confirmed):** Software developer. New to YouTube.

**Still to confirm — three questions that shape the whole course (DRAFT until answered):**

1. **Channel focus & the real why** — build an audience for your own software/SaaS, teach coding, personal/dev brand, document projects, or a creative hobby. _(The outcome matters more than the topic.)_
2. **Success in ~6 months** — comfortable with the full pipeline / a subscribers + watch-hours target / one video that lands a job or clients.
3. **Constraints** — rough weekly time budget, any gear/software budget, and the big fork for a dev channel: **on camera, or faceless** (screen-recording + voiceover).

**Working default (used until you say otherwise):** a **faceless coding/tech channel** — screen recording plus voiceover — aimed first at **pipeline fluency** (publish a handful of real videos and be comfortable with the whole plan → record → edit → publish loop), with monetization as a later, explicit goal.

## Verified facts this course is grounded on

- **YouTube Partner Program (ad revenue) threshold:** 1,000 subscribers **and** 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months, **OR** 1,000 subscribers and 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. Source: YouTube Help (official). The "500 subscribers" number from many blogs is a *separate, lower fan-funding tier*, not full ad monetization.

## Teaching stance

- **Developer analogies first.** Bridge each new idea to something you already know (a video is a "deploy," the thumbnail+title is your "landing page," retention is your "funnel").
- **Mental model before mechanics.** Understand *why* before touching software.
- **Short, single-win lessons.** One tangible result each.
- **Faceless-friendly.** Nothing assumes you must be on camera unless you choose to be.

## Out of scope (for now)

- Paid ads / promotion
- Multi-person production teams
- Brand-sponsorship negotiation (revisit once there's an audience)
